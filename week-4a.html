Introduction

VY Canis Majoris is a star well known for being one of the largest in our galaxy. It belongs to the constellation Canis Major, and has no known planets orbiting it. It is around 1000 light years away from earth and is of the spectral class M- surprisingly the same as Proxima Centauri, which despite being much much smaller, is a similar temperature of around 3400K. Though unlike Proxima Centarui, VY CMa is a red hyergiant. Far far larger than the other star.

History and properties

VY Canis Majoris is said to once have been a blue supergiant star- though now has significantly cooled and has overtime, lost mass. It was first obsereved by french astronomer Jerome Lalande in 1801, and has been watched for years- astronomers able to observe its dimming from as far back as 1850! VY CMa is said to be around 8.2 million years old and is 1500 times the width of the sun. If you put it in the suns place, its diametre would go all the way to Jupiter, and our planet and others would be engulfed by its sheer width. Now, how does a star this big even form? Hypergiant stars actually have the same start to life as any other star- with molecular clouds. These clouds, being incredibly cool, clump together. Overtime, the joining of these clouds gradually strengthens their gravitational force, causing them to eventually collapse in on eachother- and due to friction, causes it to heat up and form a protostar (baby star!). Eventually, the temperature reaches 15 million K, allowing nuclear fusion to begin and marking the birth of a star and its entry into the main sequence.
