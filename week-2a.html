Introduction

Betlegeuse is the second brightest star in the constellation orion. Orion includes the asterism orions belt, which includes other bright stars. An asterism is a recognisable pattern of stars in the sky. It is 700 light years away from earth, and is apart of the winter hexagon- an asterism of stars in winetrs northern hemisphere. Inside the winter hexagon is thw inter triangle- a smaller asterism containing our star Betelgeuse, Procyon and Sirius. Star Type and Spectroscopy

Betelgeuse is a red supergiant. Red meaning it is cooler, just like Proxima Centauri, and supergiant telling us that it is far larger and luminous than giant stars. Betelgeuse is around 10 million years old- younger than our sun by quite a lot, yet it is brighter by around 14,000 times, and has a solar mass of around 15. Despite all of this, its cooler than the sun since its a red supergiant, its surface temperature being around 3500 K. Contrastingly its core is far far hotter than its surface. Its reddish colour is a common characteristic among red supergiant stars in their final stages of stellar evolution, and its theorised it will make a supernova explosion and form a neutron star once it reaches the end of its life. It has a sun like metallicity, meaning it has similar proportion of heavy elements as the sun. Betelgeuse's Dimming

From November 2019 to May 2020, Betelgeuse's visible spectrum of light started to dim. In the early phases of this discovery, scientists proposed the idea that there was a cooler, massive 'dark spot' on Betelgeuse- however, the Hubble telecope proved that this was not the case. Over several months of Hubble's ultra-violet light observations showed that a dense, heated material travelled through Betelgeuses atmosphere. It was 2-4 times more luminous than the star- so of course, when it moved away and out through the atmospehre, the star dimmed. Alongside this, its suspected its outflow cooled and caused the formation of a darker and cooler dust cloud, which then caused the star to 'dim' by blocking its light. No astrophage troubles here!
