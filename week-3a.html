Introduction

Kepler-16b is notoriously famous for being a circumbinary planet- a planet which orbits two suns. It was first discovered way back in 2011, and is apart of the binary star system of Kepler-16. Its two suns are called Kepler-16A and Kepler-16B.

How was it discovered?

The Kepler space telescope first detected the new planet in the Kepler-16 system, two orbiting stars which eclipse eachother from our point on earth- when Kepler-16A (the larger star) gets blocked by Kepler-16B and vice versa. Scientists realised the brightness from the system dipped even when stars weren't blocking eachother- called teriary and quaternary eclipses- which hinted at there being a third object which caused this. These events happened at irregular time intervals, which further hinted that the third object was orbitting both stars.

The method for its discovery was the transit method- a common way of discovering exoplanets. A transit occurs when a planet passes between a star and its observer- seen when Kepler-16b passed by one of its suns during an orbit, dimming its light in a different manner than during the eclipsing binary of Kepler-16A and B. This dimming is often presented in light-curve graphs. Transits are very usful, as they help find out other characetristics of a planet such as its size, atmospheric conditions, temperature and orbital period.
